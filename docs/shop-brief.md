# Job sheet: thermal pad for a 13-inch M5 MacBook Air

For the repairer. One page, about 20 minutes of work, fully reversible.

Status: not planned. The owner decided on 14 November 2026 to leave his Air unmodified.
This sheet is kept for builders who choose the optional finned-plate upgrade.

## What this is

The M-series MacBook Air has no fan. Its chip sits under a thin metal shield with a gap
of about 1–1.5 mm of air between the shield and the bottom case. A thermal pad in that
gap lets heat pass into the bottom case, which will sit on a cooling tray. This is a
well-known modification on the M1, M2, M3 and M4 Air.

## Supplied by the owner

- Thermal pad, silicone, electrically non-conductive, 1.0 mm and 1.5 mm sheets (for
  example Thermalright Extreme Odyssey, 12.8 W/mK).

## The job

1. Power the Air off. Remove the bottom case (four pentalobe screws, then the clips).
2. Disconnect the battery before touching anything else.
3. **Photograph the whole interior from directly above with a ruler in frame**, before
   any change. The owner needs this to see where the chip sits.
4. Find the shield over the main chip on the logic board, near the hinge.
5. Cut a piece of pad to cover the flat top of that shield, roughly 40 × 40 mm. Keep it
   on the shield only: not on the battery, not over connectors or flex cables.
6. Start with the 1.0 mm pad. Refit the bottom case and check two things: the pad shows
   a clear contact print when the case is lifted off again, and the case sits flat with
   no bulge and all clips seated. If there is no contact print, use the 1.5 mm pad and
   check again. If the 1.5 mm pad makes the case bow or hard to close, go back to 1.0 mm.
7. Photograph the pad in place with the ruler, then close up and reconnect the battery.

## Please do not

- Remove or disturb the factory shield, tape or thermal paste.
- Use liquid metal, graphite sheet or any conductive pad.
- Put pad material anywhere on or beside the battery.

## Hand back

- Both photographs, the thickness fitted, and the unused pad.
